## Speckless SE Role-Based Coaching Context
*Source: Internal metrics session, March 6, 2026. Use this section to calibrate coaching tone, emphasis, and KPIs per SE role.*

### Corporate SE (e.g., Olajumoke Ganiyu)
**Primary metric:** Number of brand partners introduced into corporate supermarkets — NOT order volume.
- Corporate supermarkets operate as chains; a brand listed at one head office triggers orders across multiple branches. Each placement has an outsized multiplier effect.
- Evaluated weekly (not daily): track brand partner status per store — *introduced / listed / confident to list soon / no interest*.
- Uses Google Sheets + corporate survey for tracking. Performance reviews are weekly, not daily.
- Key coaching angles:
  - Depth of relationship with category managers and buyers
  - Conversion rate from "introduced" to "listed"
  - Brand spread across corporate store chains
  - Follow-up consistency to avoid product neglect
- **Do NOT coach on order count or daily visit volume.** Her role is strategic entry, not daily order generation.
- Coaching phrase template: *"The measure of this week is how many brand partners are now actively stocked in corporate stores — let's look at that number and the pipeline behind it."*

### Senior SE (e.g., Joseph Femi, Toba Meafhase)
**Primary metric:** Zone performance — brand spread in non-corporate supermarkets, debt collection, and the field output of junior SEs under them.
- Senior SEs visit **up to 5 stores per day** — their role is oversight and relationship management, not high-volume store coverage.
- They are **accountable for junior SEs' results in their zone**. A junior who misses targets reflects on the senior's rating.
- Key responsibilities: assigning juniors to regions, ensuring merchandising quality, chasing stock issues, debt collection.
- Rating dimensions:
  1. Brand spread across orders in their zone
  2. Debt collected (financial accountability)
  3. Timely order resumption across the zone
  4. Quality of junior SE supervision
- **Coaching angles for Seniors:**
  - How well are they mentoring juniors? Are juniors in their zone hitting 7-store targets?
  - Debt collection progress this week — specific amounts and stores
  - Are there stockout or expired product issues they need to escalate?
  - Brand partner introductions to key supermarkets in their zone
- Coaching phrase: *"Your score this week is partly the sum of your zone's results. How are your juniors tracking — and what have you done this week to level them up?"*

### Junior SE / Sales Executive
**Primary metric:** Daily store visits (minimum 7), early resumption (before 9:00 AM), order raising, merchandising quality, and survey completion.
- Must check into the first store **before 9:00 AM**.
- Visit **minimum 7 stores per day** — this is a hard floor, not a target.
- Core field activities: merchandising (shelf visibility, cleanliness, stock levels), raising orders to prevent out-of-stock, and filling survey reports.
- Also responsible for **market intelligence**: reporting products sitting in stores unsold, competitive pricing observations, and flagging issues to their senior.
- **Coaching angles for Juniors:**
  - Was the 9 AM first-store target met?
  - How many stores visited vs the 7-store floor?
  - Brand coverage per store — which brands were skipped and why?
  - Survey completion quality — are reports feeding the chain of command?
  - Market intelligence submitted this week
- Coaching phrase: *"Your job is simple but non-negotiable: in the store before 9, 7 stores minimum, every brand pitched, every survey filed. Which of those four slipped today and how do we fix it tomorrow?"*

### Trial SE (e.g., Ngoyo Blessing Thomas)

- Coaching emphasis: **onboarding progress** — are they learning the product catalog, survey system, and store route?

## Sales Coaching Frameworks

### GROW Model (Whitmore, 1980s)
A 4-stage structured coaching conversation. Most effective for weekly 1:1s and post-day debriefs.

| Stage | Purpose | Example questions for field SEs |
|-------|---------|----------------------------------|
| **Goal** | Clarify what they want to achieve | "What does a strong day look like for you tomorrow?" / "What's the one number you want to hit this week?" |
| **Reality** | Objective assessment of where they are | "What actually happened today — walk me through store by store." / "What brands did you miss and why?" |
| **Options** | Surface what they could do differently | "What's one thing you haven't tried with those stores?" / "Which brands could you pitch first to get momentum?" |
| **Will** | Commit to a specific action | "What will you do differently tomorrow?" / "Which two brands are you going to push first thing?" |

Key principle: Ask open-ended questions ("what", "how", "why"). Listen more than you talk. Avoid giving the answer — let the SE discover it.

Sources: [Revenue Nomad – GROW for Sales](https://www.revenuenomad.com/post/effective-sales-coaching-questions-grow-model) · [Klozers – GROW Sales Coaching](https://www.klozers.com/grow-sales-coaching-model/)

---

### SBI Feedback Model (Center for Creative Leadership)
Situation → Behavior → Impact. Forces specific, observable, non-judgmental feedback.

**Template:**
> "On [SITUATION: specific day/place], when you [BEHAVIOR: exact observable action], it [IMPACT: effect on results/team/customer]."

**Field sales examples:**

- *Positive:* "Yesterday at MegaMart, when you pitched the Zayith display stand to the category manager, it resulted in a shelf placement we've been trying to get for 3 months."
- *Gap:* "Today across your 9 stores, you only placed orders for 6 of our 24 brands. That means we're missing ₦X in potential revenue from stores that already know us."
- *Late start:* "This morning you checked in at 9:47 AM. That's 47 minutes of selling time gone — at your average order rate, that's roughly 2 missed orders."

**SBI+ variant** — add a fourth step: Intent inquiry. "What was going on for you that day?" — this prevents defensiveness and surfaces obstacles.

Sources: [CCL – SBI Feedback](https://www.ccl.org/articles/leading-effectively-articles/closing-the-gap-between-intent-vs-impact-sbii/) · [MindTools – SBI Tool](https://www.mindtools.com/ay86376/the-situation-behavior-impact-feedback-tool/)

---

### WOOP Framework (Oettingen, NYU)
Wish → Outcome → Obstacle → Plan. Combines positive visualization with obstacle anticipation. Especially effective for SEs who set intentions but don't follow through.

Use at end of coaching session:
1. **Wish:** "What do you want to achieve tomorrow?"
2. **Outcome:** "How would it feel if you hit that? What's the best result?"
3. **Obstacle:** "What might get in your way — inside yourself or in the field?"
4. **Plan:** "If [obstacle] happens, then I will [specific action]."

## Performance Psychology Principles

### Self-Determination Theory — Ryan & Deci (2000)
Three universal psychological needs that drive sustainable motivation. When all three are met, people show higher performance, lower burnout, and stronger commitment.

| Need | What it means | How to activate for field SEs |
|------|--------------|-------------------------------|
| **Autonomy** | Feeling ownership of their choices | Let them choose the order they visit stores; ask "how would YOU approach this?" rather than dictating; recognize their individual strategies |
| **Competence** | Feeling effective and growing | Celebrate specific skills — "your pitch on Kezia Foods worked because you led with the margin story"; point to measurable progress week-on-week |
| **Relatedness** | Feeling connected to the team | Reference team standings; celebrate group wins; frame missed brands as letting the team down on coverage, not just their own numbers |

Research: Autonomy support at work increases performance by 20% and job satisfaction by 43% (Journal of Organizational Behavior).

Sources: [SDT.org](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/theory/) · [Rochester Medical Center – SDT](https://www.urmc.rochester.edu/community-health/patient-care/self-determination-theory) · [PMC – SDT at Work](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9088153/)

---

### Locke & Latham Goal-Setting Theory
Specific, challenging goals outperform vague "do your best" goals. Four conditions for goals to drive performance:

1. **Specificity** — "Visit 12 stores and pitch Fabfresh to all of them" beats "try harder"
2. **Difficulty** — Goals should stretch but be attainable; too easy = no effort, too hard = abandonment
3. **Commitment** — SE must accept the goal; ask "does that feel achievable to you?" not just assign it
4. **Feedback** — Goals only work if the SE can track progress in real time

Field application: Always end coaching with a specific number target for tomorrow, not a general instruction.

---

### Growth Mindset — Dweck (2006)
Fixed mindset: talent is fixed, failure = identity. Growth mindset: ability develops through effort, failure = data.

**Fixed mindset signals to watch for in SEs:**
- Blaming external factors ("the stores don't want to buy today")
- Avoiding brands they've previously failed to place
- Taking rank drops personally rather than analytically

**Language shifts that activate growth mindset:**

| Fixed mindset phrase | Growth mindset reframe |
|---------------------|----------------------|
| "I'm not good at pitching [Brand X]" | "I haven't cracked [Brand X] yet — what's worked for others?" |
| "I always struggle with morning check-ins" | "My check-in time has been a problem — what would a one-week experiment look like?" |
| "I lost that order because the buyer was in a bad mood" | "What can I control next time? Let's focus there." |

**Coaching language:** Praise process over outcome. Say "the way you persisted with that buyer across 3 visits paid off" rather than "you're a natural salesperson." This builds durable motivation.

Sources: [Neuroleadership Institute – Growth Mindset Feedback](https://neuroleadership.com/your-brain-at-work/feedback-strategies-growth-mindset/) · [ISP – Growth Mindset in Sales](https://www.the-isp.org/sales-starts-in-the-mind-cultivating-a-growth-mindset-for-sales-success/)

## Feedback Delivery Techniques

### By performance tier:

**High performers (top 3 ranked):**
- Risk: complacency, coasting on past wins
- Technique: raise the bar with stretch questions — "You hit 22 brands today — what would it take to get to all 24 tomorrow?"
- Acknowledge mastery + challenge them to teach others: "You've figured out the Sooyah pitch — could you document what works so we can share it?"

**Mid-range performers (rank 4–10):**
- Risk: plateau, invisible to feedback
- Technique: close the gap with specifics — show exactly what separates them from rank 3
- Frame as proximity not deficit: "You're 4 points from the top 3. Here's the exact lever: brand coverage."

**Underperformers (bottom rank or declining trend):**
- Risk: shame spiral → disengagement
- Technique: separate the person from the performance; use reattribution ("this was a hard day, not a hard person")
- Lead with one strength before addressing gaps
- Use SBI+ intent inquiry before judgment
- End with one achievable action for tomorrow — not a list

## Handling Underperformance

### Reattribution Technique (Weiner's Attribution Theory)
People attribute failure to either internal/controllable causes (effort, strategy) or external/uncontrollable ones (luck, difficulty). Coaches should gently redirect external attributions to controllable ones without dismissing real obstacles.

- If SE says: "The stores just weren't buying today" → Ask: "Which stores did you break through on? What did you do differently there?"
- If SE says: "I couldn't cover more brands, the route was too long" → Ask: "Which brands would give the highest return for a 10-minute addition to your route?"

### Implementation Intentions (Gollwitzer, 1999)
If-then planning bridges the gap between intention and action. Meta-analysis across 94 studies showed medium-to-large effect (d=0.65) on goal follow-through.

**Application in coaching:** At the end of each session, have the SE complete this template:

> "If I arrive at [store name] tomorrow, then I will immediately pitch [Brand X] before doing anything else."
> "If I feel tempted to skip a store due to traffic, then I will call ahead and commit to an order by phone instead."

The specificity of the if-then plan anchors the behavior to a real-world trigger, making it near-automatic.

Sources: [SalesCubes – Implementation Intentions](https://salescubes.com/implementation-intentions/) · [PMC – If-Then Planning](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4500900/)

## Motivational Triggers for Field Sales

Research consistently shows these four levers drive field rep performance:

| Trigger | What it activates | How to use in coaching messages |
|---------|------------------|--------------------------------|
| **Autonomy** | Ownership, self-direction | "How do YOU want to approach this? What's your plan?" — don't just assign, invite strategy |
| **Recognition** | Pride, reputation | Name specific wins publicly; tell them *why* something they did worked |
| **Territory pride** | Ownership mindset | Frame their route as *their territory* — "your stores", "your customers know you" |
| **Peer comparison** | Competitive drive | Use rank and team averages to create proximate, visible competition — not shame |
| **Visible impact** | Purpose | Connect store orders to company revenue; "your ₦450K today was 18% of total team value" |

Sources: [SalesScreen – Autonomy in Sales](https://www.salesscreen.com/blog/sales-goal-tracking-and-autonomy/) · [Aircall – Intrinsic vs Extrinsic Sales Motivation](https://aircall.io/blog/sales/extrinsic-vs-intrinsic-sales-motivation-factors/)

## High-Impact Coaching Phrases

Curated for field sales coaching messages. Use near-verbatim or adapt to the SE's situation.

**For declining trend:**
- "A bad day is data, not a verdict. What does the data tell us about tomorrow?" — *effect: reframes failure as information, not identity*
- "You've done [X] before — what was different then?" — *effect: activates competence memory, breaks learned helplessness*

**For missed brands:**
- "Every brand you skip is a store visit someone else will convert. Which one can you not afford to miss tomorrow?" — *effect: frames coverage gaps as competitive opportunity, not compliance*
- "Pick your two highest-margin missed brands and make them the first calls of the day — everything else follows." — *effect: reduces overwhelm by narrowing focus*

**For late starts:**
- "Your first check-in sets the rhythm for the whole day. What's one thing you can change tonight so tomorrow starts at 8:30?" — *effect: shifts focus to controllable preparation, not the failure itself*

**For strong performers:**
- "You've cracked something that's working — what exactly did you do with [Brand X] that got that result?" — *effect: builds conscious competence, reinforces repeatable behavior*
- "You're ranked #[N]. The gap to #[N-1] is [X] points — mostly brand coverage. That's the unlock." — *effect: gives a specific, proximate target rather than vague encouragement*

**For rebuilding confidence:**
- "Focus on what you can control: first store by 8:45, pitch all 24 brands at the first three stops. Everything else is noise." — *effect: reduces cognitive load, creates clear path forward*
- "One strong day resets momentum. Make tomorrow that day — and make it specific: [store], [brand], [outcome]." — *effect: creates a concrete reset point*

**For effort recognition:**
- "The 12 stores you hit today didn't happen by accident — that's discipline. Now let's attach results to that discipline." — *effect: validates effort while redirecting to output*

**Universal closer for coaching messages:**
- "Tomorrow: [one specific action]. That's all. Everything else follows from that." — *effect: reduces overwhelm, creates a clear first step*
